Series "Open Veins of the Colombian Health System"

Pharmaceutical sovereignty:
good diagnosis,
half-implemented

Colombia imports 7 out of 10 medicines that it consumes. To change that there is a law, a CONPES, and COP 1.37 trillion committed through 2035. Here is what they promise, where the money goes, and why—almost two years after the law— Observamed considers that what has been achieved is partial and insufficient.

Dr. Óscar Iván Andía Salazar Medicines Informatics, Health and Consumer Bulletin · Updated June 24, 2026

The essentials

What matters, in six points

  • Law 2386 of 2024 declares the pharmaceutical sector strategic for the country and orders that what is now imported be produced domestically.
  • CONPES 4170 of 2025 provides the funding: COP 1.37 trillion between 2026 and 2035.
  • The diagnosis is harsh: of 6,090 marketing authorizations to manufacture and sell, only 12 —0.2%— belong to State entities.
  • Both documents diagnose the problem well. Observamed expressly acknowledges this.
  • But the law has gone almost two years without being regulated and implementation of the CONPES, about to turn one year old, is still partial.
  • The proposal: start with the information system —SITDIS—, because without data there is no way to monitor anything.

The two regulations

What each one says

One sets the framework. The other provides the money.

Law

Law 2386 of 2024

July 25, 2024
“By which national capacities in pharmaceutical research, innovation and development are strengthened”
  • Declares the pharmaceutical sector strategic for the country
  • Proposes economic incentives and promotion of specialized human talent
  • Orders the modernization of regulatory institutions, starting with INVIMA
  • Promotes international cooperation and technology transfer
  • Prioritizes care for neglected diseases
  • Establishes strict quality controls and pharmacovigilance
  • Sets out a plan to reduce external dependence and the risk of shortages
Text and analysis: Official text U. Rosario summary ConsultorSalud
Policy document

CONPES 4170 of 2025

October 30, 2025
“For strengthening domestic production” of essential drugs and medical technologies
  • Invests COP 1.37 trillion to mitigate dependence on external suppliers
  • It runs between 2026 and 2035, seeking equitable access to essential technologies
  • Expands the infrastructure of public or mixed plants and modernizes their equipment
  • Prioritizes knowledge transfer and funding for research and development
  • Aims to create strategic medicines, vaccines and essential medical technologies
  • Improves the country's response to public health crisis
Text and analysis: Centro de Estudios Regulatorios ConsultorSalud

What both have in common

Three objectives and four key points

Both documents point to the same goal. Here is what they seek and how they intend to achieve it.

Main objectives

01 · Reduce dependence

Reduce Colombia's dependence on imports of medicines, active ingredients and raw materials.

02 · Ensure supply

To prevent shortages of essential medicines and health technologies in future emergencies or public health crises.

03 · Stimulate local industry

Encourage domestic production with high standards of quality, safety, and efficacy.

Key points

Scope of application

It applies to all scientific, regulatory, promotion, production and marketing activities in the pharmaceutical sector.

Financing and incentives

Health Research Fund (FIS), tariff benefits, development loans, and financial support for scientific personnel.

Cooperation and transfer

International alliances and technology transfer to train specialized human talent in science and health.

Institutional coordination

Requires joint work by MinSalud, MinCiencias and INVIMA with academia and the private sector.

Department

70% of the medicines consumed are imported. Colombia imports 8 times more than it exports.

State participation

Of 6,090 marketing authorizations (registros sanitarios) to manufacture and sell, only 12 belong to State entities. That is 0.2%.

R&D gap

Colombia invests 0.31% of GDP in research and development. The OECD average is 2.7%.

Social return

In the base scenario, the present value of the benefits is COP 5.91 trillion, with a benefit/cost ratio of 6.01.

Progress and timeline

How the ten years break down

Three stages between 2026 and 2035. Most of the investment arrives around the midpoint.

2026 – 2027

Enablement phase

Start of «turnkey» contracting processes, technical designs, and future-appropriation procedures.

2030 – 2031

Peak investment

Period of highest budget execution, with more than COP 800 billion allocated to infrastructure and equipment.

2032 – 2035

Consolidation and closure

Final GMP/GLP certifications, process validation, and final impact report on health sovereignty.

Vecol · Vaccines

Interventions covering 4,500 m², BIM modeling, international certifications, and world-class standards.

U. de Antioquia

Solids and liquids. Interventions across 700 m² with BIM modeling and international certifications.

National Institute of Health

IV fluids. Interventions across 4,700 m² under world-class standards.

Natl. Cancer Institute

Biotechnology products, under world-class standards. And district projects such as Bogotá Bio.

Observamed's take

What Observamed thinks

This is the bulletin's opinion, and we want to state it as such: it is an interpretation, not official data.

“Notable in its diagnostic components and its proposals. But its regulation and implementation are partial and insufficient.”

DiagnosisBoth documents identify the problem and its causes well.
RegulationAlmost two years after Law 2386, it still has serious problems.
ImplementationPartial and insufficient, even counting CONPES 4170.

The bulletin's conclusion is not to stop at criticism: civil society organizations have an obligation to contribute, and the proposed starting point is the design of the Integrated Transactional and Distributed Health Information System (SITDIS). Without reliable information, no control is possible.

In the meantime, there is something you can do today

Public policy moves at its own pace, and that takes years. But what the pharmacy charges you can be checked today, and it costs nothing. Here is how:

Enter the active ingredient or the brand name of the medicine—preferably including the strength—and start the search.
The available options appear with their prices online.
Learn the difference between “pioneer brands,” “competitor brands,” and “genuine generics”.
Compare the prices reported by laboratories with what pharmacies charge.
Rate the best option and assess the honesty of the intermediation before placing your order.
An exorbitant price does not mean better quality. It is usually the result of poor commercial practices.
