The Council of State (Consejo de Estado) ruling on medicine prices

The Council of State (Consejo de Estado) confirmed that the State violated collective rights through failures in price policy, oversight and medicine supply, and ordered the creation of a new national pharmaceutical policy. Here we explain what it says, what it orders and how compliance is progressing.

View document →

The case in a threadThe tutela (constitutional injunction), message by message

  1. In 2019, the Office of the Inspector General (Procuraduría) sued the State through a class action (acción popular): according to the lawsuit, medicine price controls cover a very small part of the market and the rest is hardly monitored (pp. 2-5).
  2. Sued the Ministry of Health, the Ministry of Commerce, the National Commission on Medicine Prices, the National Health Superintendency (Supersalud), and the Superintendency of Industry and Commerce; INVIMA, ADRES, and the Comptroller General's Office were later joined to the case (pp. 1, 10-11).
  3. The Medical College of Cundinamarca and Bogotá joined the case as a supporting party in March 2024 (p. 11).
  4. On December 12, 2024, the Administrative Tribunal of Cundinamarca found three proven problems: failures in price policy, in oversight and control, and in medicine supply (pp. 12, 15).
  5. The national pharmaceutical policy (CONPES 155 of 2012) expired in 2021 and no new one was shown to exist. For that reason the Tribunal ordered a working group to build one (pp. 12, 45-47).
  6. The Ministry of Health, INVIMA and the superintendencies appealed. On September 17, 2026, the Council of State (Consejo de Estado) confirmed almost the entire ruling (pp. 1, 70-73).
  7. Only one thing changed: the new policy may take the form of a CONPES or another suitable legal instrument (pp. 53, 71).
  8. And it added something that was missing: a committee to oversee compliance with the ruling, chaired by the Tribunal magistrate (p. 73).
  9. One of the nine topics the roundtable must study is a price data system that, under the SISMED model, is public, reviewable and dynamic (p. 72).
  10. What comes next: forming the working group within the month following notification, setting up the verification committee and receiving the Ministry's progress reports. We will report each step here (pp. 72-73).

To understand itThe ruling in plain words

The Office of the Inspector General (Procuraduría) sued the State because, as it stated, medicine price controls in Colombia cover only a small share of products and the rest is hardly monitored. It claimed this raises public procurement costs and what people pay out of pocket (pp. 4-5, 7).

The Administrative Tribunal of Cundinamarca partly agreed with the plaintiffs on December 12, 2024: it found three proven problems, failures in price policy, in oversight and control, and in medicine supply (p. 15). It ordered the creation of a working group to build a new national pharmaceutical policy, because the previous one (CONPES 155 of 2012) ceased to be in force in 2021 (pp. 12, 45, 47).

Several entities appealed. The Council of State (Consejo de Estado) upheld almost everything, corrected one point (the new policy does not have to be a CONPES document: it may be adopted through another suitable legal instrument) and added what was missing: a committee to oversee compliance with the ruling (pp. 57, 70-73).

Second instance, September 17, 2026What the Council of State (Consejo de Estado) decided

Modifies

Working group for a new pharmaceutical policy

The working group led by MinSalud, the CNPMD and INVIMA continues, with the same participants and nine points of study. The policy may take the form of a CONPES or another suitable legal instrument. It must be established within the month following notification; MinSalud chairs it and submits progress reports to the magistrate (pp. 71-72).

Adds

Compliance verification committee

New. It is chaired by the reporting judge (magistrado sustanciador) of the Tribunal and is made up of the Office of the Inspector General (Procuraduría), a representative with decision-making power from each defendant and joined entity, and the Public Ministry. The judge may add other entities (p. 73).

Confirms

Everything else in the 2024 ruling

The declaration of violation of collective rights and the orders to MinSalud and INVIMA (Urgent Response Plan against shortages), to the SIC (sanctioning proceedings), to the National Health Superintendency (Supersalud) (proceedings over diversion of funds and audits of IPS), and to ADRES (reimbursement procedures with a report within three months) remain in force (pp. 13-14, 70, 73).

In addition

Costs, Ombudsman's Office, and case file

There is no order for costs; a copy is sent to the Ombudsman's Office (Defensoría del Pueblo); once the ruling becomes final, the case file returns to the court of origin (p. 73).

Remain in forceThe orders to each entity

EntityWhat it must do
MinSalud and INVIMAContinue implementing the Urgent Response Plan for the crisis of medicine and supply shortages (order 2.2, p. 13).
Superintendency of Industry and CommerceContinue the sanctioning proceedings under the price control regime, including recoveries and reimbursements, and study whether its team is sufficient for the task the law assigned to it (order 2.3, pp. 13-14).
National Health Superintendency (Supersalud)Advance administrative proceedings when it finds diversion of resources through overcharging, and audit the IPS in which an EPS holds more than 50% ownership, to determine whether medicines were paid for above the maximum price (order 2.4, p. 14).
ADRESBegin the proceedings for the monetary reimbursement of income obtained from possible overcharges in medicine prices, against 16 companies and individuals (laboratories, pharmacies, cooperatives, and IPS) named in the order (order 2.5, p. 14).

Order 2.1The nine topics the working group must study

  1. Reformulate the price control policy for medicines and medical devices, guaranteeing access for the population; free competition is not an absolute right.
  2. Analyze market regulation strategies: lower-priced generics, decentralized purchasing, reverse auctions or public procurement, and framework price agreements.
  3. Create or strengthen a data system with the inventory and price of medicines; modeled on SISMED, it could be redesigned to be public, reviewable, and dynamic.
  4. Implement the Electronic Invoicing Control System.
  5. Concrete solutions against shortages, including strengthening INVIMA to issue marketing authorizations (registros sanitarios) in less time.
  6. Design and implement the national policy on pharmaceutical research, development, innovation and production for health sovereignty.
  7. Patents and industrial property: manual updates.
  8. To evaluate the financing and deficit-remediation instruments of the health system (reimbursement claims, maximum budgets, the final-settlement agreement).
  9. Schedule of meetings and activities.

Why it matters to the ObservatoryPrices, data and transparency

Public, reviewable and dynamic SISMED

One of the points the roundtable must study is an inventory and medicine price data system that, under the SISMED model, could be redesigned to be public, reviewable and dynamic (pp. 13, 72). It is the type of information the Medicines Observatory analyzes and publishes.

Reliable and public price information

The ruling recalls that the first strategy of the 2012 pharmaceutical policy sought reliable, timely and public information on prices, to address problems of transparency and information asymmetry (p. 48).

Reporting to SISMED is also a consumer right

The Chamber cites its own ruling of March 26, 2026, which protected consumers' rights in a case of failure to report data to SISMED (p. 36).

An ally of the Observatory took part in the process

The Medical College of Cundinamarca and Bogotá was admitted as a supporting party on March 13, 2024 (p. 11).

Observatory ReadingWhat the ruling gets right

Links pricing and supply

It treats medicine shortages and price speculation as a single problem, and gets INVIMA and the Price Commission working together.

It does not stay in the future

In addition to the new policy, it maintains specific orders for the SIC, the National Health Superintendency (Supersalud) and ADRES regarding overcharges already paid.

Opens the table to society

Patients, academia and civil society get a seat at the table in building the policy, not just the Ministry.

Observatory ReadingThe risks to its success

Paralysis by analysis

A roundtable with dozens of stakeholders with opposing interests can prolong discussions without timely decisions.

Capacity and budget

The orders demand more technical capacity from INVIMA, the SIC and the Superintendency, but the ruling does not allocate resources.

A problem with many causes

Shortages also depend on raw materials and global logistics, which court orders alone cannot resolve.

PolicyComplexResistance is expected over the renegotiation of margins, patents and the recovery of funds by ADRES.

What comes nextWhat remains to be fulfilled

  • Form the working group within the month following notification and inform the magistrate of its members (p. 72).
  • Set up the compliance verification committee (p. 73).
  • Management and progress reports from MinSalud to the presiding justice (p. 72).
  • The ruling does not state the date of notification; for that reason the dates of these deadlines cannot yet be set.
