Glossary

The observatory's words,
explained

Bulletins use technical terms that not everyone needs to know. Here are all 116, with what they mean and why they matter.

PreMaxRef The prices

Maximum Reference Price

The ceiling set by the State: the most that can be charged for that exact presentation of a medicine.

Why it matters. It is the central column of the doctor's tables. When that ceiling is miscalculated, an overcharge becomes legalized.
Also appears as: PMV · Maximum Sale Price
IPS margin The prices

Margin for the healthcare provider institution

The additional percentage that the clinic or hospital dispensing the medicine may charge.

Why it matters. In the tables it is exactly 3.50% for expensive medicines and 7.00% for cheap ones. This is useful for checking that a figure has been read correctly.
PRA The prices

Applicable Regulated Price

The final authorized price: the maximum reference price plus the institution's margin.

Why it matters. It is what is actually paid in the end. It must always be PreMaxRef + Margen IPS.
PPPS The prices

Average Price of the Presentation in SISMED

What the laboratory actually sold, on average: the total invoiced divided by the units sold.

Why it matters. If your entity buys, it should ask for discounts on this value. It is the real price, not the list price.
PPPEFI The prices

Average Retail Price Surveyed in Online Pharmacies

What the medicine costs at the pharmacy, according to a direct survey.

Why it matters. It is exclusive to the VMI-CFN System. Placing it next to the PPPS shows the complete journey of the price.
Validation mesh The prices

Validation mesh

Automatic filter in an information system that should detect and reject impossible or erroneous data.

Why it matters. If it fails, SISMED accepts price reports containing errors.
Entry prices The prices

Entry price regulation

Setting or reviewing the price of a new medicine at the moment it reaches the market, not afterward.

Why it matters. The bulletin notes that this strategy was left out of the six the Ministry developed.
Also appears as: prices at market entry and by therapeutic value
Per capita The prices

Per capita value

Payment of a fixed amount for each person served, regardless of how many services they use.

Why it matters. The Ceilings would give the EPS a per capita payment for procedures.
CUM The codes

Unique Medicine Code (Código Único de Medicamento)

The identification number that INVIMA assigns to each medicine: exact brand, strength, presentation and laboratory.

Why it matters. Two boxes with the same name but different sizes have different CUMs. That is why price is verified by CUM and not by name.
Also appears as: CUM Of · CUMO
ATC The codes

Anatomical, Therapeutic, Chemical Classification

The World Health Organization system that groups medicines by what they are used for and how they act.

Why it matters. Lets you look at an entire family at once. ATC4-A10B, for example, covers all non-insulin antidiabetics.
Also appears as: ATC4 · ATC-5
ARMI The codes

Med-Informática Reserve Archive

The copy the observatory keeps of each official document at the moment it is published.

Why it matters. If an entity changes or removes a file from its website, the observatory keeps the original version.
Also appears as: ARmi
CNPMyDM The entities

National Commission on Medicine and Medical Device Prices (CNPMyDM)

The State body that decides the maximum a medicine can cost in Colombia.

Why it matters. Its circulars are the ones the observatory audits: when the Commission makes a mistake, the error becomes law.
INVIMA The entities

National Institute for Drug and Food Surveillance

The authority that approves which medicines may be sold in the country and monitors their quality.

Why it matters. Assigns the CUM codes and marketing authorizations (registros sanitarios). Without its approval, a product cannot legally circulate.
SISMED The entities

Medicine Price Information System

The public database where laboratories are legally required to report how much they sell and at what price.

Why it matters. It is the source of almost all of the observatory's figures. Its quality depends on someone reviewing it.
IPS The entities

Health Services Provider Institution

Clinics, hospitals, and medical centers: those who treat the patient and deliver the medicine.

Why it matters. This is the party that is granted the additional margin on top of the maximum reference price.
EPS The entities

Health Promotion Entity (Entidad Promotora de Salud)

The insurer that manages its members' health funds and contracts with IPS.

Why it matters. It is the large buyer of medicines. Its ability to negotiate prices determines a good part of spending.
MiPres The entities

Prescription platform

The system where physicians must record prescriptions for medicines that are not covered by the benefits plan.

Why it matters. Cross-referencing MiPres with SISMED reveals whether more is being sold than is being prescribed.
ADRES The entities

Administrator of the Resources of the General Social Security Health System

The State entity that manages health funds: it receives the resources and pays the EPS, clinics and providers.

Why it matters. Reimbursement claims, maximum budgets and direct payment all pass through its hands. The doctor demands that it publish the data on what it pays.
MinSalud The entities

Ministry of Health and Social Protection

The ministry that leads the country's health policy and issues the resolutions on medicines and prices.

Why it matters. It is the entity that opens procedures such as the declaration of public interest and is responsible for the SISMED and MiPres information systems.
Also appears as: Ministry of Health
UPC The entities

Capitation Payment Unit (UPC)

The fixed sum that the system pays the EPS each year for each member, to cover what is included in the benefits plan.

Why it matters. What the UPC does not manage to cover is paid separately (reimbursement claims (recobros) or maximum budgets (presupuestos máximos)), and large sums have gone there.
IETS The entities

Institute for Health Technology Assessment

An institute that assesses whether a new medicine or treatment works better than what already exists and how much it is worth paying for it.

Why it matters. Law 1753 of 2015 intended its assessment to be used to set the price of new medicines from the moment they enter the country.
OTIC The entities

Information and Communications Technology Office

The Ministry of Health office in charge of information systems, including SISMED.

Why it matters. The doctor states that he does not validate or analyze the reports, and that is why SISMED shows impossible figures.
AFIDRO The entities

Association of Pharmaceutical Research and Development Laboratories

The trade association that groups multinational pharmaceutical companies in Colombia.

Why it matters. In 2018 it asked the new Government to halt several price regulations, and the doctor documents that those requests were granted.
CVCS The entities

Committee for Citizen Oversight and Health Cooperation

Civil society organization that monitors the health system and holds the government to account.

Why it matters. In 2023 it asked the Minister of Health to correct the failures in the information systems documented by the Observatory.
OMS The entities

World Health Organization

The United Nations health agency. It publishes treatment guidelines and the Model List of Essential Medicines.

Why it matters. Its recommendations serve as a reference: for example, dolutegravir is its preferred treatment for HIV.
Also appears as: OPS/OMS
SIC The entities

Superintendency of Industry and Commerce

The entity that monitors competition among companies and protects consumers; it also handles patents.

Why it matters. In 2018 it halted the circular that would have set entry prices for new medicines.
Council of State (Consejo de Estado) The entities

Council of State (Consejo de Estado)

The highest court for cases against the State: it reviews whether the Government's decrees and resolutions comply with the law.

Why it matters. It suspended Decree 710 of 2018 and in 2026 issued the ruling on medicine price policy that the Observatory follows.
High-Cost Account (Cuenta de Alto Costo) The entities

High-Cost Account (Cuenta de Alto Costo)

Technical body of the health system that monitors the costliest diseases, such as HIV, cancer and kidney disease.

Why it matters. It should have data on HIV patients, but the doctor notes that it does not publish information on dolutegravir.
Also appears as: CAC
Comptroller's Office The entities

Office of the Comptroller General of the Republic (Contraloría General de la República)

State entity that oversees how public resources are spent and managed.

Why it matters. This is the unit that detected the overcharges and the concentrations of money described in these notes.
Also appears as: Comptroller (its highest authority)
DIARI The entities

Directorate of Information, Analysis and Immediate Reaction

Unit of the Comptroller's Office that analyzes data to detect alerts on the use of public resources.

Why it matters. This is the unit that found the alleged overcharges in medicines.
Vertical integration The entities

Vertical integration

When an EPS has business ties with the IPS or providers it contracts and pays them preferentially. The law limits this to prevent concentration and abuse.

Why it matters. The Comptroller's Office detected possible violations at four EPS.
EAPB The entities

Benefit Plan Administrative Entities (Entidades Administradoras de Planes de Beneficios)

Entities, such as the EPS, that administer health insurance and receive money from the system to guarantee care for their enrollees.

Why it matters. Only 11 reported how they used the maximum budgets; the other 28 still had to report.
National Health Superintendency (Supersalud) The entities

National Health Superintendency (Supersalud)

State entity that oversees and controls the EPS, clinics, and other actors in the health system.

Why it matters. Worked with the Ministry on the detailed tracking of the invoices of the Glosa Transversal.
VMI-CFN From the observatory

Med-Informática Vademecum and National Pharmaceutical Catalog

The observatory's database: pharmacological, regulatory and economic information on the medicines available in Colombia.

Why it matters. It is the only system that brings together on one screen what the laboratory reports, what the State regulates, and what the pharmacy charges.
BisCoM From the observatory

Health Informatics and Medicine Consumer Newsletter

The observatory's weekly publication, with an ISSN since 1991.

Why it matters. Each edition carries a number and the week it covers. It is the archive where all this research lives.
Also appears as: BIS
SITDIS From the observatory

Integrated Transactional and Distributed Health Information System

The information system the observatory proposes for cross-referencing all of the sector's data.

Why it matters. It is its central thesis: without data that can be cross-referenced, spending cannot be controlled.
ASPEEC From the observatory

Analysis, Synthesis, Projection, Execution, Evaluation and Corrective Measures

The methodology of the observatory's special investigations.

Why it matters. It does not stop at the diagnosis: it proposes corrective measures and then evaluates whether they were applied.
APP-I From the observatory

Primary Price Analysis via the Internet

The version of the VMI-CFN system that compares the laboratory's price with the pharmacy's. Free for the first month.

Why it matters. It is the tool of Operación Transparencia Farmacéutica, open to health professionals.
Medicines Observatory From the observatory

Medicines Observatory of the Colombian Medical Federation

It is a group within the Colombian Medical Federation (Federación Médica Colombiana), the professional association that brings together physicians' organizations across the country. It studies and publishes information on medicines, their prices, and their use in Colombia.

Why it matters. Its analyses documented the inconsistencies in dolutegravir sales, price and prescription data that are set out in the letter sent to the minister.
Also appears as: Colombian Medical Federation (Federación Médica Colombiana)
Pioneer brand The medicines

The original

The laboratory that developed the molecule and was first to launch it on the market.

Why it matters. It is usually the most expensive even when the patent has expired and identical alternatives exist.
Genuine generic The medicines

The same medicine without a brand

It is sold under the active ingredient name and meets the same INVIMA quality requirements.

Why it matters. Same molecule, same dose, up to four times lower price. That difference is the observatory's central finding.
Also appears as: Competing brand
Medical sample The medicines

Product with no commercial value

The medicine that the laboratory gives free to the doctor so that they become familiar with it. By definition, it is not sold.

Why it matters. The observatory found 33 of them with an assigned regulated price, some of them in the millions. If they have no commercial value, they should not have a price ceiling.
GLP-1 The medicines

Glucagon-like peptide-1 agonists

A family of medicines for type 2 diabetes that also reduce weight. It includes semaglutide and liraglutide.

Why it matters. They moved COP 2.9 trillion in Colombia between 2016 and 2025.
Also appears as: Glutidas
SGLT2 The medicines

Sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors

Antidiabetics that cause sugar to be eliminated in the urine. Dapagliflozin, empagliflozin.

Why it matters. It is the group that moved the most money: COP 3.6 trillion in ten years.
Also appears as: Gliflozins
DPP-4 The medicines

Dipeptidyl peptidase-4 inhibitors

Another family of oral antidiabetics. Sitagliptin, linagliptin.

Why it matters. COP 1.3 trillion over the period analyzed.
Also appears as: Gliptins
Irbesartán The medicines

Irbesartán

Medicine used to control high blood pressure.

Why it matters. Its price and that of its partner, amlodipine, serve as an example to show that the combination of the two had a much higher cap than the sum of each one.
Amlodipino The medicines

Amlodipino

Medicine used to treat high blood pressure and some chest pain of cardiac origin.

Why it matters. Its separate price is added to that of irbesartan to calculate how much the combination should cost.
Fixed combination The medicines

Fixed combination of medicines

Product containing two or more active ingredients in the same tablet or presentation, always in the same doses.

Why it matters. The bulletin says the reimbursement ceilings for these combinations are among the most inconsistent.
Also appears as: two-drug combination
Nusinersen The medicines

Nusinersen (Spinraza)

High-cost medicine used to treat spinal muscular atrophy, a neuromuscular disease of genetic origin.

Why it matters. More than COP 1,123 million was reimbursed for just 3 vials for one patient.
Also appears as: Spinraza
Emicizumab The medicines

Emicizumab (Hemlibra)

Medicine used to prevent bleeding in people with hemophilia A.

Why it matters. The bulletin cites it because it entered the market at COP 29 million per vial without price regulation.
Also appears as: Hemlibra
Ocrelizumab The medicines

Ocrelizumab (Ocrevus)

Medicine used to treat multiple sclerosis, a disease that attacks the nervous system.

Why it matters. The bulletin cites it because it entered the market at COP 17 million per vial without price regulation.
Also appears as: Ocrevus
Antivirals The medicines

Antivirals

Medicines that fight infections caused by viruses.

Why it matters. The declaration of public interest for hepatitis C antivirals had been in process for 4 years.
DIP Laws and procedures

Declaration of Public Interest

A Ministry of Health decision declaring that access to a medicine is of public interest, usually because it is very expensive or scarce.

Why it matters. It is the first step toward a compulsory license or toward lowering its price. Dolutegravir has been in that process since 2023.
Also appears as: declaration of public interest
Compulsory license Laws and procedures

Compulsory license

Permission granted by the State for others to manufacture or import a patented medicine without the patent owner's authorization, in exchange for a royalty.

Why it matters. It opens the door to much cheaper versions. Colombia has tried several times without success.
Voluntary license Laws and procedures

Voluntary license

Agreement in which the pharmaceutical company that owns the patent allows others to manufacture generic versions, almost always only for certain countries.

Why it matters. ViiV Healthcare signed it for dolutegravir in other countries, but Colombia is not included.
Patent Laws and procedures

Patent

Exclusive right that a company holds over an invention, usually for 20 years: while it lasts, no one else can sell the same medicine.

Why it matters. When the patent expires, generics can enter the market and the price should fall.
Recobro (reimbursement claims) Laws and procedures

Recobro (reimbursement claims)

What the EPS bills the State (today ADRES) for medicines or services that were not covered by the UPC.

Why it matters. Exorbitant prices were paid through reimbursement claims; the doctor says they should not be used to calculate caps.
Also appears as: reimbursement claims (recobros)
VMR Laws and procedures

Maximum Reimbursement Values

The caps that the Ministry of Health set on what EPS could claim back from the State for each medicine.

Why it matters. Some ended up far above the real price, such as the irbesartan and amlodipine combination.
Maximum Budgets Laws and procedures

Maximum Budgets (ceilings)

A sum that ADRES transfers in advance each year to each EPS to cover what the UPC does not pay; the EPS bears the risk if it falls short.

Why it matters. They were created by Law 1955 of 2019. The doctor warned that they would be unworkable and that with them the trail of many prescriptions was lost.
Also appears as: ceilings
Benefits Plan Laws and procedures

Health Benefits Plan (PBS)

The list of services and medicines that the EPS must provide to its members with UPC funds.

Why it matters. If a medicine is in the plan, the pharmaceutical company usually uses it as an argument that its price does not need to be lowered.
Also appears as: PBS
NoPBS Laws and procedures

Services not included in the Benefits Plan

The medicines and services that are not in the benefits plan and are paid separately, through reimbursement (recobro) or maximum budgets (presupuestos máximos).

Why it matters. They are the ones whose spending has grown the most. The doctor warns that the ceilings could create a new "NoNoPBS" group with no one to pay for it.
Also appears as: NoNoPBS
Billing objection (glosa) Laws and procedures

Billing objection (glosa) and Cross-Cutting Billing Objection

An objection to a healthcare invoice or billing statement (glosa): what has been disputed is not paid until it is clarified. The "Glosa Transversal" of 2019 was a mechanism for paying pending reimbursement claims (recobros) as public debt.

Why it matters. With it, hundreds of billions of pesos were recognized as public debt.
Also appears as: Cross-Cutting Billing Objection (Glosa Transversal)
Direct payment (giro directo) Laws and procedures

Direct payment (giro directo) and advance payment

When ADRES pays the clinic or provider directly, without the money passing through the EPS.

Why it matters. It aims to ensure that money reaches whoever provides the service; its new reporting mechanism starts in 2027.
Also appears as: prior transfer (giro previo)
PND Laws and procedures

National Development Plan

The law containing each government's program for its four-year term; it often includes articles on health and medicines.

Why it matters. Law 1753 of 2015 and Law 1955 of 2019 are development plans that changed medicine regulation.
Marketing authorization (registro sanitario) Laws and procedures

Marketing authorization (registro sanitario)

INVIMA's permit to manufacture, import and sell a medicine in Colombia.

Why it matters. Law 1753 of 2015 sought to assess therapeutic value during that registration, but a decree separated the two.
Therapeutic value Laws and procedures

Therapeutic value

How much a new medicine truly improves patients' health compared with the treatments that already exist.

Why it matters. Setting the price according to therapeutic value avoids paying fortunes for medicines that are no better.
Constitutional (exequible) Laws and procedures

Constitutional (exequible)

When the Constitutional Court decides that a law is consistent with the Constitution and remains in force.

Why it matters. The Court declared Article 72 on therapeutic-value pricing constitutional (exequible), yet it was still not applied.
Fiscal Oversight and Control Plan (Plan de Vigilancia y Control Fiscal) Laws and procedures

Fiscal Oversight and Control Plan (Plan de Vigilancia y Control Fiscal)

Comptroller's Office program that defines which entities and resources it will audit during the period.

Why it matters. It will include audits of 28 EPS in the second half of the year.
Subsidized regime Laws and procedures

Subsidized health regime

Form of health enrollment for people who cannot afford to pay, financed mainly with State resources.

Why it matters. Five EPS received about 44% of the money (UPC) of this regime.
Contributory regime Laws and procedures

Contributory health regime

Form of health enrollment for people who have employment or income and contribute a portion of it.

Why it matters. Serves as a comparison: it received COP 14.73 trillion in UPC over the same period.
Monthly Settlement of Enrollees (Liquidación Mensual de Afiliados) Laws and procedures

Monthly Settlement of Enrollees (Liquidación Mensual de Afiliados)

Monthly calculation that determines how much money (UPC) each EPS in the subsidized regime receives based on its enrollees.

Why it matters. This is the source of the figures on how much the five EPS received.
Final Settlement Agreement (Acuerdo de Punto Final) Laws and procedures

Final Settlement Agreement (Acuerdo de Punto Final)

Mechanism for the State to pay overdue debts of the health system to EPS and providers.

Why it matters. Only COP 1.51 trillion of the projected COP 6.7 trillion had been paid.
Also appears as: Punto Final (Final Settlement)
National Pharmaceutical Policy Laws and procedures

National Pharmaceutical Policy

Colombian State policy that brings together strategies so people have access to medicines at reasonable prices.

Why it matters. Of its ten strategies, the Ministry developed only six.
Public debt Laws and procedures

Public debt

Money the State acknowledges it owes and commits to pay.

Why it matters. Decree 1350 of 2019 recognized as public debt up to COP 222,654 million for the Glosa Transversal (cross-cutting billing objection).
Comparability Guide Laws and procedures

Comparability Guide

Technical document that defines how to show that a new biotechnological medicine is comparable to one already approved.

Why it matters. It was one of the tasks still pending from the 2014 Biotechnology Decree.
Risk Management Plans Laws and procedures

Risk Management Plans

Plans describing how a medicine's risks will be monitored and reduced once it is on the market.

Why it matters. They were another pending task of the 2014 Biotechnology Decree.
GSK Companies and brands

GlaxoSmithKline

British multinational pharmaceutical company. It handles its HIV medicines through ViiV Healthcare.

Why it matters. It sells dolutegravir in Colombia (TIVICAY, TRIUMEQ, DOVATO) and the doctor anticipates that it will oppose the compulsory license.
ViiV Healthcare Companies and brands

ViiV Healthcare

Company specializing in HIV medicines, majority-owned by GSK.

Why it matters. It is the holder of dolutegravir and signed a voluntary license for other countries, not for Colombia.
MPP Companies and brands

Medicines Patent Pool

United Nations-backed organization that negotiates voluntary licenses with pharmaceutical companies so that low-cost generics can be manufactured in low- and middle-income countries.

Why it matters. Thanks to it, generic dolutegravir is available in many countries, but Colombia was left out.
TIVICAY Companies and brands

TIVICAY (dolutegravir)

Brand name of dolutegravir 50 mg, an HIV medicine.

Why it matters. It is GSK's number one product in Colombia by value sold, and its regulated price is almost four times the one the laboratory reports.
TRIUMEQ Companies and brands

TRIUMEQ (dolutegravir + abacavir + lamivudine)

Brand name of a pill that combines three HIV medicines, one of them dolutegravir.

Why it matters. Its regulated price ended up far above what the laboratory reports and what it costs in pharmacies.
DOVATO Companies and brands

DOVATO (dolutegravir + lamivudina)

Brand name of a pill that combines two HIV medicines.

Why it matters. It entered without a regulated price.
KALETRA Companies and brands

KALETRA (lopinavir + ritonavir)

Brand name of a combination HIV medicine.

Why it matters. A declaration of public interest was requested for it and denied: this is the precedent weighing on the dolutegravir case.
GLIVEC Companies and brands

GLIVEC (imatinib)

Brand name of imatinib, a medicine for a type of leukemia (chronic myeloid leukemia).

Why it matters. It was the first case of a declaration of public interest in Colombia: there was no compulsory license, but the price was lowered.
ISENTRESS Companies and brands

ISENTESS (raltegravir)

Brand name of raltegravir, another HIV medicine.

Why it matters. Its maximum price is triple what the laboratory reports: the problem is not limited to a single manufacturer.
DOXOPEG Companies and brands

Doxopeg (pegylated liposomal doxorubicin)

Injectable chemotherapy medicine used, among other things, for ovarian cancer.

Why it matters. This is the medicine that was reportedly reimbursed at up to 8,000% above the maximum price.
Also appears as: DOXOPEG (pegylated liposomal injectable suspension 20 mg/10 mL)
HAEMATE P Companies and brands

Haemate P 500 UI

Medicine that provides clotting factors (factor VIII and von Willebrand factor) to treat von Willebrand disease.

Why it matters. Up to 6,000% above the maximum permitted price would have been claimed for reimbursement.
Also appears as: HAEMATE P 500 UI
IMMUNATE Companies and brands

Immunate

Baxter medicine that provides clotting factors to treat von Willebrand disease.

Why it matters. Up to 3,000% above the maximum permitted price would have been claimed for reimbursement.
Also appears as: IMMUNATE 1000
Biotoscana Companies and brands

Biotoscana

Pharmaceutical company that markets medicines in Colombia.

Why it matters. This is the company associated with HAEMATE P, which was flagged for exceeding the maximum price.
Baxter Companies and brands

Baxter

Multinational company of medical products and medicines.

Why it matters. This is the company associated with IMMUNATE, which was flagged for exceeding the maximum price.
Sanofi-Aventis Companies and brands

Sanofi-Aventis

Multinational pharmaceutical company with a presence in Colombia.

Why it matters. According to the bulletin, it shares a technical team with Genzyme and Genfar, which made the same reporting error.
Also appears as: Sanofi
Genzyme Companies and brands

Genzyme

Pharmaceutical laboratory that is part of the Sanofi group.

Why it matters. Their sales reports to SISMED showed figures that look like errors.
Genfar Companies and brands

Genfar

Laboratory that sells generic medicines and is part of the Sanofi group.

Why it matters. According to the bulletin, it shares the technical team that made the reporting error.
Winthrop Companies and brands

Winthrop

Pharmaceutical brand of the Sanofi group, under which the amlodipino mentioned in the article is sold.

Why it matters. Its price of COP 19,680 is one of the two figures the bulletin uses to calculate the combination.
Biogen Companies and brands

Biogen

Pharmaceutical company that makes Spinraza.

Why it matters. Their medicine appears as an example of exorbitant costs in reimbursement claims (recobros).
Roche Companies and brands

Roche

Multinational pharmaceutical company of Swiss origin that makes Hemlibra and Ocrevus.

Why it matters. Two of its medicines are examples of high entry costs without regulation.
Pfizer Companies and brands

Pfizer

Multinational pharmaceutical company of US origin.

Why it matters. The bulletin mentions it as one of the two multinationals that are in fact the same company, along with Mylan.
Mylan Companies and brands

Mylan

Multinational pharmaceutical company focused mainly on generic medicines.

Why it matters. Appears alongside Pfizer among the multinationals that own competitors of the biotechnological products.
VIH Health and treatments

Human Immunodeficiency Virus

The virus that attacks the body's defenses. Without treatment it can lead to AIDS; with daily treatment, a person can live many years.

Why it matters. Treatment is lifelong, so the price of antiretrovirals weighs heavily on the country's spending.
Also appears as: AIDS, AIDS
Antiretroviral Health and treatments

Antiretroviral

A medicine that stops HIV inside the body. It is taken every day and almost always combined with others.

Why it matters. It is a permanent expense for the system, which is why its price matters so much.
Also appears as: antiretrovirals
Dolutegravir Health and treatments

Dolutegravir

A modern antiretroviral for HIV, recommended by the WHO as the preferred treatment since 2019.

Why it matters. Between 2013 and 2022 it accumulated more than COP 287 billion in sales in Colombia, and a declaration of public interest is in process.
Also appears as: DTG
MABs Health and treatments

Monoclonal antibodies

Biologic medicines manufactured in a laboratory that attach to a specific target, for example a cancer cell. Their names end in "-mab", such as pembrolizumab.

Why it matters. They are among the most expensive medicines: four of them, for cancer, spent about COP 3.2 trillion in ten years.
Also appears as: MABS, monoclonal antibodies
Biotechnology products Health and treatments

Biotechnological (biologic) medicines

Medicines produced with living cells, such as vaccines or monoclonal antibodies. They are more complex and costly than an ordinary pill.

Why it matters. The rules for allowing its cheaper versions (biosimilars) onto the market have been widely debated in Colombia.
Also appears as: biologics
High cost Health and treatments

High-cost medicines

Treatments that cost millions of pesos per patient, such as those for cancer, rare diseases or hemophilia.

Why it matters. A few patients can consume a huge share of the budget if their price is not regulated at market entry.
Hepatitis C Health and treatments

Hepatitis C

Infection of the liver caused by a virus. Today it is cured with new antivirals, but those medicines were very expensive.

Why it matters. Colombia processed a declaration of public interest to lower the price of those antivirals.
Active ingredient Health and treatments

Active ingredient

The substance in the medicine that produces the effect; the rest of the pill is filler ingredients. Several brands can have the same active ingredient.

Why it matters. Comparing by active ingredient shows how much each brand charges for the same thing.
Also appears as: active ingredients
B24X Health and treatments

B24X code

The International Classification of Diseases code for "HIV disease, unspecified".

Why it matters. Almost all dolutegravir prescriptions in MiPres use that generic code, which says little about the patient.
Primary and secondary transactions Health and treatments

Primary, secondary and final sales (SISMED)

In SISMED, primary sales are those of the laboratory; secondary sales are those of intermediaries (wholesalers and distributors) to pharmacies and institutions; and final sales are those that reach the patient.

Why it matters. If secondary sales far exceed primary sales, intermediaries would be selling more than they bought: a sign of erroneous data.
Microdata Health and treatments

Microdata

The data record by record (each payment, each prescription), not just the totals.

Why it matters. Without them, no one can verify whom ADRES pays and why.
Traceability Health and treatments

Traceability

Being able to follow the path of a medicine or a prescription step by step, from the prescriber to the recipient.

Why it matters. With maximum budgets (presupuestos máximos), the trail of many dolutegravir prescriptions was lost.
Citizen oversight Health and treatments

Citizen oversight

A group of organized citizens that monitors how public resources are used and holds the State accountable.

Why it matters. An oversight group (veeduría) brought to the Minister of Health the information failures documented by the Observatory.
Pegylated liposomal suspension Health and treatments

Pegylated liposomal injectable suspension

A way of presenting a medicine: the active ingredient is placed inside tiny fat particles (liposomes) coated so they last longer in the body, and it is administered by injection.

Why it matters. Describes the DOXOPEG presentation, whose price is regulated.
Also appears as: 20 mg/10 mL indicates the amount of medicine per vial
Von Willebrand disease Health and treatments

Von Willebrand disease

A coagulation disorder, usually hereditary, in which the blood does not clot properly and bleeding is prolonged.
